1# Integer division of an `i64` number by the `i64` number `b`, truncated
2# toward zero. The quotient is built by restoring long division over the
3# magnitudes of both operands, with the sign applied afterwards. When `b` is
4# zero, the caller-supplied `cant-divide` fallback is returned, and so it is
5# when the smallest `i64` is divided by minus one, since that quotient is one
6# past the largest `i64` and has no answer here. The magnitude of the
7# dividend is taken once, since the restoring loop reads one bit of it
8# per step and negating a negative dividend costs a full carry chain.
9#
10# @todo #8385:60min Make an i64 division affordable. Starting the loop at the
11# highest bit of the dividend brought `42 / 10` from 234MB to 85MB, but what
12# is left is not in this loop. Measured on their own, `i64.plus` allocates
13# 4MB while `i64.neg` allocates 168MB, and `minus`, `gt` and `lt` cost
14# 170-220MB each because they are built on `neg`. A division of small
15# numbers still costs tens of megabytes, so `1.5.as-fixed 2` still cannot
16# run under a 300MB heap. Until that is cheaper, the tests below stay over
17# the one second that `Deadline` allows and are recorded as skipped instead
18# of being run.
