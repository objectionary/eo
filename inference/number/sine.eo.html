1# Sine of `num` radians as `org.eolang.number`.
2# The argument is reduced into the [-pi, pi] range and the Taylor series is
3# summed over the reduced angle through Horner's scheme.
4#
5# The reduction takes `turns` whole turns off the angle, and the accuracy of
6# the answer is the accuracy of that one subtraction: at `1e15` the reduced
7# angle is what survives of a number sixteen digits long, so a product of
8# `turns` and two pi rounded into a single double keeps none of the digits
9# that decide the sine. The product is therefore carried in two doubles.
10#
11# `tau` is the nearest double to two pi and `tail` is what that double
12# misses. `turns times tau` is taken exactly by Dekker's method: `gap` is
13# two to the 27th plus one, and multiplying by it and subtracting back splits
14# a double into halves narrow enough that their pairwise products round
15# nowhere: `turns` into `top` and `foot`, `tau` into `crown` and `stub`.
16# The four partials `high`, `mid`, `cross` and `low` then say what the single
17# rounded `product` dropped, and `slip` is that dropped part. The `angle` is
18# the argument less `product`, less `slip`, less `turns times tail`, taken
19# once and read back by the series as the `reduced` number.
20#
21# The attributes below stand in alphabetical order, which is not the order
22# they are computed in; the paragraph above is the order.
23#
24# Two doubles carry about 106 bits, so the reduction holds to a few ULP for
25# as long as `turns times two pi` needs no more than that, which is |num| up
26# to about `1e15`. Past it the reduced angle drifts out of [-pi, pi] and the
27# series is summed further from zero than it is meant to be. Holding the
28# whole range of a double would take Payne-Hanek reduction, with two-over-pi
29# written out to more bits than any product of doubles carries.
30# Non-finite arguments collapse to nan.
