1# Calculate MOD.
2# An operation that finds the remainder after dividing the number by `b`.
3# When `b` is zero, `cant-mod` takes an error message. When unbound,
4# it terminates with that message. Otherwise
5# the divisor is doubled until it reaches the dividend and then halved back
6# down, subtracting it from the dividend whenever it fits. Doubling and
7# halving a double are exact, and every subtraction happens while the
8# remainder is below twice the subtracted value, which is exact too, so the
9# answer matches the true remainder for any pair of finite numbers, however
10# far apart their magnitudes are, at the cost of two recursive steps per
11# bit of the quotient.
12#
13# Each step hands the next one a `!` attribute rather than the expression
14# that computes it. Dataization is not cached, so a chunk written as the
15# doubling of the chunk of the step above is recomputed from the first
16# doubling every time a step reads it, which costs one walk of the whole
17# chain per step and makes the whole thing quadratic in the number of
18# steps: the largest double over the smallest subnormal spent seventy
19# seconds of it (#9276). A `!` attribute is computed once, so a step reads what the
20# one above it already has, and `number` is put back around it, since what
21# a `!` attribute keeps is the bytes and not the object they came from.
22#
23# When the dividend is `nan` or infinite, the result is
24# `nan`, regardless of the divisor. Both the number and `b` have to be eight
25# bytes wide, as `plus` and `div` ask of theirs; anything else terminates.
