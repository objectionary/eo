1# Writes the ASCII `code` (0-127) as its single byte, the inverse of `as-ascii`.
2# The receiver must be an integer within the range [0, 127]; any other value
3# (negative, above 127, or fractional) terminates the computation instead of
4# being silently truncated to its least-significant byte.
5#
6# The range stops at 127 because that is where ASCII stops. A byte from `80-`
7# to `FF-` is never a character on its own in UTF-8, it is a continuation byte
8# or the lead of a sequence, so a string holding one alone is one that
9# `length`, `slice` and `at` all terminate on.
