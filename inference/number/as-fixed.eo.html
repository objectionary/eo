1# Writes the number as fixed-point decimal with exactly `places` fraction
2# digits, rounding to the nearest unit in the last place, so that `2.5` with
3# four places becomes `2.5000` and `0.9999999` with six places becomes `1.000000`.
4# If `places` is not a finite non-negative integer below 2^53 (negative,
5# fractional, nan, infinite, or too large to reach zero by repeated
6# subtraction of one), the `cant-print` fallback is returned, or the
7# terminator when unbound.
8#
9# The value is split into its integer and fraction parts before scaling, so
10# only the fraction is multiplied by a power of ten and every magnitude that
11# `as-decimal` writes exactly is written exactly here too. A magnitude of
12# 2^63 or larger is past what `as-decimal` can write, so it returns the
13# `cant-print` fallback as well, instead of terminating.
14#
15# The fraction is scaled by at most 10^15, the largest power of ten that
16# keeps the scaled value an exact integer below 2^53, and never by 10^places
17# directly. Only that many digits are peeled off; any place beyond them is a
18# trailing zero. This keeps the digits shared by two calls with different
19# `places` in agreement, instead of drifting as an unfloored accumulator was
20# carried through more divisions the larger `places` was.
