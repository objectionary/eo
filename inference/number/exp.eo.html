1# Returns Euler's number raised to the power of a `num`.
2# An argument above `710` answers `pinf` and one below `-750` answers zero.
3# Beyond those two bounds the answer is out of the range a double holds anyway.
4# In between, the argument is reduced the way libm does it: `num` is written
5# as `k ln2 + r`, with an integer `k` and `|r|` no bigger than half of `ln2`,
6# using `ln2` split into a high part whose product with `k` is exact and a
7# low part that carries the rest. Then `e^r` is a short Taylor polynomial,
8# evaluated by Horner's rule from the thirteenth term down, and the answer is
9# that polynomial scaled by `2^k`, which `power` computes exactly. The
10# scaling goes in two halves, so that neither a `k` near the top of the
11# range nor one in the subnormal range overflows or underflows on the way.
12# Raising the rounded constant `e` to `num` instead multiplies its rounding
13# error by `num`, which is 272 ULP off at `700` (#9029).
