1# Writes the number as signed decimal digits, truncating its fraction
2# towards zero, so that `42.7` becomes `42` and `-42.7` becomes `-42`.
3#
4# A magnitude below ten billion is written a digit at a time, dividing by ten,
5# which a double does without losing anything while the magnitude stays below
6# `2^53`. Above ten billion the magnitude is split at the tenth digit first,
7# and each half is written the same way.
8#
9# The split is exact for every magnitude a double holds below `2^63`. `top` is
10# below `2^30`, so it is an integer a double keeps whole; ten billion is
11# `2^10` times `5^10`, so `top` times ten billion is a multiple of `2^10`, and
12# a double spaces its values no wider than that anywhere below `2^63`, so the
13# product is exact too; and `rest` is what is left, below ten billion, which
14# makes the subtraction exact because its answer is representable. Splitting at
15# the ninth digit instead would leave the product only a multiple of `2^9`,
16# half a step off the `2^10` grid the topmost binade sits on. Dividing by ten
17# billion is not exact, so `guess` can land one either side of the true
18# quotient, and the sign of `slack` says which way to step it back.
19#
20# `rest` is written with a leading ten billion and the leading digit dropped,
21# since the ten digits it stands for are ten digits whether or not they start
22# with a zero.
23#
24# The sign of an exact negative zero is kept, as `as-fixed` and `as-scientific`
25# keep it, so `-0.0` becomes `-0` and not `0`. A negative number whose fraction
26# is truncated away loses its sign, since `-0.5` is written as the `0` it
27# truncates to.
