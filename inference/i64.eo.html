1# Signed 64-bit integer.
2# Here `as-bytes` must be a `bytes` object.
3#
4# The out-of-bounds message of `as-i32` names the receiver with `%s` over
5# `as-decimal`, not with `%d`, because `%d` would take a `number` and the
6# `i64` to `number` conversion is lossy at the top of the 64-bit range: the
7# nearest double of `9223372036854775807` is `2^63`, which is one past the
8# `long` range that `printf`'s own `%d` guard accepts, so the message itself
9# used to terminate; `as-scientific` rounds the mantissa to six digits (#9725).
10#
11# Negation is two's complement, one `plus` over the flipped bits, and not
12# `times` by minus one. Every bit of minus one is set, so the shift-and-add
13# loop of `times` ran a full addition on all sixty four of them, which is
14# what `minus`, `lt`, `lte`, `gt` and `gte` all rest on.
15#
16# `as-number` splits the two's complement into its top twelve and low
17# fifty-two bits and reads each half through the exponent pattern of
18# `2^52`: a double with that exponent and a mantissa `m` is exactly
19# `2^52 + m`, so one `or` and one subtraction turn a half into an exact
20# number. The two halves are then combined with one addition, which
21# rounds to the nearest even double, the same way a JVM `long` becomes a
22# `double`; a negative value is read as the negated complement of its
23# halves, so that no carry has to be propagated through the bytes.
24#
25# `plus` reads both operands through the same pattern as two thirty-two
26# bit halves, adds the halves as numbers, and carries from the low half
27# into the high one with a single comparison, so an addition costs the
28# same few dispatches whatever the length of its carry chain; the bytes of
29# each sum below `2^32` are the low four bytes of its `2^52` pattern.
