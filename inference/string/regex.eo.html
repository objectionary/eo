1# Regular expression in Perl format.
2# The pattern is the string it is taken off.
3# It starts and ends with slash (e.g. "/(your-expression)/"),
4# Also it can be specified by the flag option,
5# e.g.
6# ```
7# (`Q.string.regex "/(word)/i").compiled.matches "WORD"
8# ```
9#
10# There are two entry points and one letter between their names.
11# `matches` answers whether the whole text matches: the block it finds has
12# to start at zero and end at the length of the text, so a pattern that
13# covers only part of it answers `false`.
14# `match` searches instead: it hands back a cursor that holds no block of
15# its own, whose `next` is the first block found anywhere in the text, and
16# the `next` of a block is the one after it.
17# ```
18# (`Q.string.regex "/[a-z]+/").compiled.matches "1abc" # false
19# ((`Q.string.regex "/[a-z]+/").compiled.match "1abc").next # block "abc"
20# ```
21#
22# Supported flags:
23# /d - Enables Unix lines mode.
24# /i - Enables case-insensitive matching.
25# /x - Permits whitespace and comments in pattern.
26# /m - Enables multiline mode.
27# /s - Enables dotall mode.
28# /u - Enables Unicode-aware case folding.
29#
30# The engine is written here, in EO, on top of the byte primitives.
31# `compile` reads the expression: the slashes, the body between them and
32# the flag letters after the closing one. What comes out is a `pattern`
33# holding the body and the flags; `checked` hands the same pattern back
34# when the body parses and applies the fallback to the construct the
35# parser choked on and to the character offset it sits at otherwise, worded
36# as `java.util.regex` words it, so `/a**/` is refused with "Dangling meta
37# character '*'" at offset 2 whatever the flags are.
38#
39# The parser walks the body as bytes, since every metacharacter is ASCII
40# and a multi-byte character is copied whole, and turns it once into a tree
41# of nodes with the flags baked in. It understands literals, the escapes
42# `\\ \/ \. \t \n \r \f \a \e \xHH` and `\Q...\E`, classes with ranges,
43# negation and `\d \D \s \S \w \W`, the dot, the anchors `^ $ \A \z \Z \b
44# \B`, the quantifiers `* + ? {n} {n,} {n,m}` with a lazy `?` or a
45# possessive `+` behind them, alternation, capturing and `(?:)` groups,
46# the inline flags `(?idmsux-idmsux)` and `(?flags:...)` scoped the way
47# Java scopes them, and `/x` comments. Lookaround, named groups, back
48# references, atomic groups, `\uHHHH`, `\p{}`, class intersections and
49# the remaining escape letters are refused at compile time with a message
50# saying so. A quantifier binds to the last character of a literal run,
51# also when that run came out of `\Q...\E`, as in Java.
52#
53# Every node has a `run` that takes the subject, a byte position, the
54# captures found so far and a continuation, and either applies the
55# continuation to the position it stops at or answers `nothing`, so
56# alternation and quantifiers backtrack by returning to the caller. The
57# `/i` flag folds ASCII; with `/u` it also folds Latin-1, Greek and
58# Cyrillic letters and treats `i`, `I`, the dotless i and the dotted
59# capital I as one letter. `\b` looks at ASCII word characters only.
60# Positions in the answer are counted in characters, as before, while the
61# engine counts bytes and converts at the border; a matched block remembers
62# where its bytes end, so `next` neither rescans the text nor parses the
63# pattern again.
64#
65# @todo #7485:30min Stop skipping 'redundant-attachment' here. It complains
66# about three formations, the fallback of `compiled`, the continuation
67# `sought` hands to `run` and the one the parser builds for `\z`, and reads
68# their `>>` names as unreferenced. At least the one in `sought` does reach
69# out of itself, through the receiver its own head declares, and the lint
70# misses it, because a void of the same name masks the reference. Dropping
71# the names breaks the engine and everything built on it, `string.scanf`,
72# `string.as-number` and `path` among them. Remove this directive once
73# objectionary/lints#1301 counts a hop through the receiver as reaching
74# outward even when the head declares a void of the same name.
